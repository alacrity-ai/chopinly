## There is no save button

Every committed edit is written to this device at once — a placement, a delete, a retype, the drop of a drag. Close the tab mid-bar and it is already there. Reopening restores the piece and the zoom you left it at.

That is the whole of it without an account: your compositions live **in your browser, on your device**, and never leave it.

## The library

`‹` from the editor is the compositions list.

![The compositions list.](/img/help/compositions-list.png)

- **new** starts one; **import** reads MusicXML files, several at a time — see [MusicXML in and out](help:musicxml).
- **Search** by title, composer or tag. Sort by recent, title or composer, and group by composer.
- **Tap** a row to open it. **Hold** a row for its details — title, composer, tags — and to delete it.

The details sheet is the same one that greets a new composition. The title also sits in the editor's header: tap it there.

## With an account

Sign in with an emailed code — there is no password — and your compositions sync like everything else in the logbook: your practice, your goals, your scores. Write on the iPad, open the laptop, carry on.

A composition is capped at a megabyte of music, which is about a hundred bars of solid sixteenths on both staves. The account sheet's **download everything** includes your compositions, and deleting the account wipes them with the rest.

## What stays on the device on purpose

Your zoom, which rails you show, [Pen ∣ Touch](help:input), [Gesture](help:gestures) and your [Favorites](help:favorites) are remembered **per device** and are not synced. They are habits of the thing in your hands — the iPad wants different rails from the laptop — not properties of the music.

## Linking a piece to practice

A composition can be linked to a goal, exactly as a score can, and *practice this* starts the clock on it. Your own piece becomes a thing you log hours against.
