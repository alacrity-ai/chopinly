A resting palm must never write a note. That rule shapes everything here. **Pen ∣ Touch** lives in [Options ▾](help:options) under *Input*, and it exists only on a device that can be touched — a desktop never sees it.

## Pen

The Pencil, or a mouse, draws. **A finger on the score does nothing at all** — no ghost, no tap, no grab, no lasso. It cannot interrupt a stroke in flight, and it cannot place a stray note while your hand rests on the glass.

This is deliberately absolute. An earlier version let a quick, narrow finger tap through, and that loophole is exactly where a palm got in.

The rails are separate from the score, so your fingers keep working on every button in every setting. In Pan, one finger still pans and two still pinch.

## Touch

The finger becomes the pen. Use it when the Pencil is dead, or on a phone.

- A **tap places**, however slowly you take it.
- In **Select** a finger grabs a note, rest or mark from a fingertip away — the hit targets grow to match a finger, and the nearest thing wins. In **Place** the staff is for placing, so a finger grabs a head only when it lands right on it; a fat target there would grab the note you are trying to add a third above.
- A **second finger lets go**. It is the natural cancel, and a pinch never draws.

## Hold, then slide, to aim

A fingertip covers three staff spaces, so a plain tap is a blunt instrument for pitch. Hold for half a second, or slide, and the gesture becomes an **aim**: the ghost lifts clear of your fingertip where you can actually see it, follows your finger, and lands where the ghost is when you lift.

![Hold or slide and the ghost lifts above the fingertip.](figure:gesture-aim)

Aiming works for everything the editor places — a note, an armed key or clef, a dynamic, a fingering, a paste. Lifting off the staff, a second finger, or a cancelled pointer all abandon it. And a finger that has not slid by **two seconds** is not aiming, it is summoning [Favorites](help:favorites).

## Which one you start in

A device that has never seen a pen starts in **Touch**, so a phone works from the first tap. The first time a Pencil touches the score, the switch flips to **Pen** once with a note about it. After that the setting is yours and never moves on its own — a deliberate Touch is not undone by the next pencil stroke.

The choice is remembered per device, like your rails and your zoom.
