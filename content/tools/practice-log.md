## What it does

A **goal** is anything you practise: a piece (*Beethoven — Pathétique, ii*), a technique (*scales*, *octaves*), or something else (*sight reading*). Type a name and it exists; pieces carry a composer. Press **play** and pick the goal; the clock runs on it. Switch goals mid-sitting and nothing is lost — the time so far stays on the first goal and the clock continues on the second. Press **stop** and a short flourish shows what the sitting was worth, then the minutes go into today's list and onto the calendar. Forgot to press play? Hold the button and add the minutes after the fact.

Each goal has a **notes** thread. The last note you wrote greets you the next time you press play on that goal, so "left hand bar 31, slow" is waiting where you left it. Tempos stamped from the [metronome](/metronome) build a **sparkline** on the goal's page. Takes from the [recorder](/recorder) sit on it too.

## History and analytics

**Today** shows only what you practised, with minutes per goal. **History** is a calendar of dots coloured by how long you practised each day, with a report for any day you tap and the current streak of practiced days. **Analytics** covers any range: total time and the change against the previous period, days practised, sessions, time per day; the share by **composer, work and type**; when in the day you practise, which weekdays carry the load, how long a session tends to run. Tap Bach and every chart recomputes for Bach.

Sight-singing and ear-training runs land in the log by themselves, folded into one entry per day.

## Why a log at all

Practice is invisible from the inside. You remember the good day and forget the four days you skipped; you believe you spend an hour on the hard piece and it is twenty minutes. A log you have to fill in by hand dies in a fortnight. A log that fills itself in from the play button keeps going, and after a month it tells you things — that you never practise scales on Fridays, that the sonata gets half the time the étude does, that sessions after 9 pm are short. There is a longer piece on this in the blog: [why keep a practice log](/blog/why-keep-a-piano-practice-log).

## Your data is yours

Everything lives on your device with no account at all. Sign in with an email code and it is backed up and synced between your phone and your desk. Download everything as a file, or delete everything, any time. No password, no subscription, no ads, no tracking.

## Details

- Runs in the browser; installs to the home screen; works offline and syncs when you are back.
- Free, and the source is public.
