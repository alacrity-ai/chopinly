Compose

A grand staff that takes a tap where a pen would go. Choose the value, touch the line or the space, and the note is there — engraved, in time, ready to play back, print or hand to Sibelius.

Open compose free · no sign-up · works offline

What it does

Write by tapping. Pick a note value on the rail — whole to sixty-fourth, dots, rests — and tap the staff where the note goes. It lands on that line or space at that beat, spelled for the key, with the rests around it re-grouped the way an engraver would group them. Tap the same beat again for a chord; drag a head up or down to re-pitch it; select a run and make it a triplet, a quintuplet, a tie, a slur. Accidentals, articulations, ornaments, glissandi and rolled chords are one tap on a selection. Up to four voices per staff, with notes that cross to the other staff when a passage asks for it.

Change anything anywhere. Key, time and clef changes go on the bar or the beat you tap, and the music re-flows to fit: a change to 3/4 re-cuts the bars with tied notes where they have to split. Dynamics, hairpins and text sit on beats and half-beats of their own, so they can be selected, dragged in time, lifted or lowered, and deleted like anything else.

Shape the form. A Form rail puts double and final barlines, repeats with first and second endings, segno and coda signs, D.C. and D.S. al Fine or al Coda, To Coda, Fine, rehearsal letters and tempo changes (Adagio ♩ = 60) on the bar you tap.

Mark it for the hands. A Piano rail puts a pedal line (Ped. to the lift, with retakes), an 8va or 8vb line (the notes under it draw an octave lower or higher; the pitch is untouched) and fingering — a digit on each notehead, stamped on a selection or by tapping heads with the digit armed — where you tap.

Hear it. Play, pause, stop, skip by the bar, drag the playhead, set a tempo. Playback follows the form — repeats twice, endings in turn, the jumps — and slows or quickens at each tempo mark; the pedal holds the notes under it; dynamics and hairpins shape the velocities; slurs play legato; rolled chords roll.

Keep it. Pieces save as you write and, with an account, follow you between devices. Undo reaches back two hundred steps.

Out of Chopinly

PDF. The export sheet shows page one at the staff size you choose, on Letter or A4, with the margins and a title block, then saves the file to the device or shares it. Add to Scores puts the same PDF into your Scores library with the title, composer and tags, so the piece you wrote is the piece you practise, with bookmarks and Pencil ink like any other score; send it again after a change and the library copy is replaced in place.

MusicXML. Export MusicXML writes a MusicXML 4.0 file — pitches, rhythms, chords, ties, tuplets, voices, cross-staff notes, key, time and clef changes, articulations, ornaments, dynamics, hairpins, text, tempo, pedal and octave lines, fingering — that Sibelius, MuseScore, Finale and Dorico open. The reverse works too: import a .musicxml, .xml or compressed .mxl file from any of those programs (or from a scan you had recognised elsewhere) and it becomes a composition you can edit, play and print. What Compose cannot hold is set aside and named: lyrics, chord symbols, grace notes.

Made for the glass

Compose was written for an iPad with an Apple Pencil, and works with a finger or a mouse. The pen places; a palm resting on the glass does not. Pinch to zoom, pan with two fingers, or switch to Pan mode and use one. Every button is a real square, big enough to hit while the other hand holds the keyboard's edge. Nothing is recognised or guessed: what you tap is what you get, which is the point.

Your music stays yours

Compositions live in your browser on your device. With an account they are backed up and synced like the rest of your practice; without one, nothing leaves the device. Exports and imports are files you choose — no server sees them.
